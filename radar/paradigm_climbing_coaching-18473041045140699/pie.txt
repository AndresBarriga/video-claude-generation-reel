Inside Flag: Episode 2 of “Elite Technique,” the series for climbers who want hard moves to feel suspiciously easy and their beta to look top notch.

The inside flag has been accused of bad technique. Today, we clear its name.

Also known as a “through flag,” “Can-Can,” or “Ferret Launcher” (a term I have courageously fabricated), it can help you oppose rotation and stay stable while reaching. Because ideally, only the hand you’re moving should leave the wall.

When your other leg(s) obstruct a backflag, or you need more opposition to the rotation around the hand you’re pulling from, an inside flag can be the better tool. Sometimes the answer is simply putting a leg somewhere deeply unintuitive.

However, it can also get you into a position that feels brilliant until you need to do literally anything else. Unwinding may then require a degree in human origami, and failure to graduate can result in an unscheduled departure from the climbing surface.

So close this cursed little application, retrieve your shoes and performance leggings, and test the same move with a backflag, a foot swap and regular flag, and finally an inside flag.
